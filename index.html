Industry note

  • The FCC voted Thursday to let prisons jam phones: at its November 19 open meeting, the FCC unanimously approved a rule allowing state and local prisons and jails to jam contraband cellphone signals for the first time. The rule allows — but does not require — facilities to use jamming, and each facility must apply to the FCC, complete testing showing no harmful interference outside a defined containment area, and notify the community before deployment. Florida Rep. Kat Cammack (FL-03) pushed the issue and celebrated the vote. Commissioner Anna Gomez, the lone Democrat, supported the order but dissented in part, warning about unintended consequences such as disrupted emergency calls and arguing jamming costs should not be passed to families through rates for authorized communications. Sources: FCC Unanimously Approves Prison Jamming Order, FCC allows prison and jail cellphone jamming: What that means, Cammack's Crackdown on Prison Cellphones Crosses the Finish Line at FCC
  • Nielsen is rewriting how radio audiences are counted: Nielsen told PPM stations Thursday it will drop its Average Quarter-Hour listening qualifier from three minutes to one minute, effective with the April 2027 survey period (April 1–28, results starting in May). Nielsen estimates the change will increase measurable ad impressions by about 20%. It follows the January 2025 move from a five-minute to a three-minute qualifier, and mirrors the one-minute qualifier Nielsen already applied to local TV measurement on August 31. A two-week preliminary dataset arrives in December, with a webinar to review it and a fuller impact analysis in February. The story hanging over local TV

  • The Republican Party of Florida has sued the owners of 11 stations in the Orlando and Tampa Bay markets, seeking to block a Democratic ad about the Hope Florida controversy and threatening criminal prosecution if stations air material from a leaked grand jury report.
  • Press-freedom groups say the suit threatens broadcasters' First Amendment rights, while Republicans point to an Oct. 7 court ruling ordering the grand jury report removed from court records.
  • Source: Florida GOP Sues 11 TV Stations to Block Democratic Ad
