The story hanging over local TV

  • No new court development in the Hope Florida ad fight surfaced today. The status stands as reported in the Oct. 26 edition: the First District Court of Appeal declined to block TV stations from airing the Democratic Party's Hope Florida ad, rejecting Attorney General James Uthmeier's request for an injunction, and the Republican Party of Florida voluntarily dismissed its Highlands County lawsuit against the stations on Oct. 21, a day after a judge refused its emergency request to pull the ads without a hearing. The dismissal was without prejudice, so the party could file again. Sources: Florida appeals court declines to block political ad citing Hope Florida grand jury report, Hope Florida Ad Lawsuit Dropped: Court Records Explained
  • What has not gone away: the Oct. 16 letters from two state attorneys — Brian Haas of the Tenth Circuit and Susan Lopez of the 13th Circuit — to TV station managers citing the grand jury secrecy statute. No station has been charged. Source: GOP drops Hope Florida ad lawsuit, but prosecutors' warnings still stand
  • With Election Day six days away (Tuesday, Nov. 3), expect the ad — and the fight over it — to stay in heavy rotation on Orlando stations through the weekend.

The story hanging over local TV

  • The Republican Party of Florida has dropped its lawsuit seeking to block a Democratic ad about the Hope Florida controversy from Orlando and Tampa Bay-market TV stations. The party filed a voluntary dismissal on Wednesday, Oct. 21, one day after Highlands County Circuit Judge Peter Estrada refused its emergency request to pull the ads without a hearing. The dismissal is without prejudice, so the party could file again. Sources: Florida GOP drops Hope Florida ad lawsuit, GOP drops Hope Florida ad lawsuit, but prosecutors' warnings still stand
  • The threat to stations has not fully lifted: on Oct. 16, the same day the party sued, two state attorneys — Brian Haas of the Tenth Circuit and Susan Lopez of the 13th Circuit in Hillsborough — sent their own letters to TV station managers citing the grand jury secrecy statute and urging them to talk to their lawyers. No station has been charged. Source: Florida Man News Network update
  • Central Florida Public Media's Oct. 19 report on the original suit names the Orlando-market owners involved, including WFTV, WESH, WKMG, and WOFL. Source: Florida Republican Party wants court to block Democrats' Hope Florida ads
